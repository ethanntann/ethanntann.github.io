Gamertaggd

Letterboxd and Strava for games, built and shipped solo: an iOS app, its API, a website and a Windows tracker. Live on the App Store since September 2026.

Why this

I track a lot of my life: workouts on Strava, films on Letterboxd. Games had nothing. When friends, or strangers, get competitive about games, we compare how many we've played, on what, how many we've 100%'d and how many hours we've sunk in, and nobody actually knows. So I built the app I wanted in my pocket: one true record of everything I play that I can pull up and show someone, and that uses the same data to tell me what to play next when I'm in a slump.

Key decisions

  1. One ID for every game: everything is keyed on IGDB, never a store's ID, so a session from Steam and a rating from the phone land on the same game.
  2. Imports that survive a crash: each import is a leased job in a PostgreSQL queue with limited retries and batch writes that are safe to repeat.
  3. Blend is built for groups: taste is scored on ratings, genres, shared games and completion habits for any number of players.
  4. AI that can't make games up: the model turns a request into filters, candidates come from real data, and it may only pick from them by ID.
  5. Trust the server, not the phone: Pro access is checked against RevenueCat on the server, and share pages ship with no client-side JavaScript.

Salience

A Fortnite auto-clipper for Windows that ranks highlights the way you would: 97.4% agreement with my own keep/skip ratings, for about $0.003 per 8 seconds of play.

Why this

Clipping tools either save the last 30 seconds when you press a key, which you forget mid-fight, or guess from loud audio. When I measured that on my own footage, a live trigger caught only 5 of the 15 moments I actually kept. A good clip is personal, so Salience looks at every moment after the match and learns what you keep.

Key decisions

  1. Nothing heavy during play: analysing live cost about 11 fps, so everything waits for the game to close. A ticking clock in the window caused a stutter, found with PresentMon.
  2. Look at everything, not just triggers: a vision model describes every 8-second window for about $0.003 each, chosen from 8 models at 1/20 the price of the best.
  3. Your taste, measured: a model trained on keep/skip ratings puts 97.4% of kept/skipped pairs in the right order.
  4. Keep data local: only gameplay frames leave the PC; audio, transcripts, inputs and ratings stay on it.
  5. No game hooks: capture uses the same Windows APIs as any screen recorder, at about 3% CPU.
